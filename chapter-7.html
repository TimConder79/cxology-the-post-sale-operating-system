Chapter 7

Stage 4 — Intent

Formalize commitment and establish a concrete timeline — early.

In the previous stages of the post-sales pipeline, you identified a growth opportunity, aligned it with the customer’s strategic goals, and empowered an internal champion to advocate for the solution. Now, you arrive at the critical pivot point: transitioning from theoretical interest to formal commitment.

The fourth stage of the post-sales pipeline is Intent. This stage is entirely focused on securing the customer’s commitment to move forward and establishing a concrete timeline for upgrading or renewing. Ensuring alignment on timing, approvals, and budget processes prevents last-minute surprises and delayed deals. When properly executed, formalizing intent feels like a natural next step—not a forced sales push—because the customer clearly sees the expansion as an investment in their own success.

Initiate Early Discussions The biggest mistake Customer Success and Account Management teams make is waiting until the end of a contract cycle to discuss renewals or expansions. To secure intent smoothly, you must proactively begin these conversations early.

As a best practice, you should initiate renewal conversations mere days after the kickoff by simply asking the customer when they would like to discuss their renewal. Renewals are usually much more awkward for the vendor than they are for the customer, and raising this topic right away breaks the tension and completely disarms the awkwardness of the eventual ask. By bringing up the renewal or expansion well in advance, you build a true partnership mindset and prevent last-minute obstacles from derailing the process. Your structured Alignment Meetings provide the perfect venue to keep these discussions focused, transparent, and customer-centric. During these meetings, you can map out the timeline, gain alignment on the decision-making process, and address potential roadblocks before they arise

Understand Constraints and Approval Workflows Even with a strong business case and an eager advocate, deals stall when the vendor fails to understand how the customer actually buys software. To formalize intent, you must uncover their budget limits, approval processes, and decision timelines as early as possible.

You must ask the logistical questions:

  • When does your budget cycle reset?
  • Who ultimately needs to sign off on this expansion?
  • Are there legal, procurement, or security reviews required before we can finalize?

By anticipating these constraints and understanding their internal workflows, you ensure a smooth path forward and eliminate the friction that often delays closed-won revenue.

Leveraging Advocacy for Urgency You don't have to navigate these internal workflows alone. This is where the work you did in Stage 3 pays off. You must rely on your internal advocates to drive urgency, build consensus, and navigate the maze of internal decision-makers to ensure alignment.

Because your champion knows the internal politics and priorities of their organization better than you ever could, they can guide you around potential landmines. They can tell you exactly whose approval is a formality and whose approval requires a detailed ROI presentation. By leveraging their influence, you strengthen urgency and consensus-building efforts.

Securing the Verbal Commitment The ultimate goal of the Intent stage is to obtain a verbal agreement from the customer. This verbal "yes" serves as a crucial milestone; it signals their readiness to proceed and allows both teams to shift their focus directly to finalizing contracts and securing official approvals.

Once the customer verbally commits, the posture of the relationship shifts. You are no longer proving the value of the expansion; you are simply executing the administrative steps to make it a reality.

When to Engage Sales While the Customer Success Manager (CSM) is the primary driver of value and relationship management, they do not have to be an expert in complex contract negotiations. A key practice during the Intent stage is knowing when to involve the Sales team.

For straightforward renewals or standard license additions, the CS team can often manage the process end-to-end. However, you should seamlessly loop Sales back into the conversation when dealing with intricate deal management, budget reallocations, or complex negotiations that require specialized expertise. Sales and CS should work together as a unified front to support the advocate in presenting the final business case.

By proactively initiating discussions, mapping the customer's buying process, leveraging your champion, and knowing when to bring in Sales, you smoothly guide the opportunity to the finish line.
