Chapter 5

Stage 2 — Align

Alignment is about tying your recommendations to the customer's strategic objectives — not your product roadmap.

The second stage of the post-sales pipeline is Align. Alignment is about tying your recommendations to the customer’s short-term priorities and long-term strategic objectives. Without this critical step, any attempt at an upgrade or expansion will feel like a transactional sales push rather than a valuable, strategic recommendation.

Customers do not buy more software simply because they can—they buy because they see a direct, undeniable path to value. To build a business case that resonates, you must continuously clarify their goals, document them for total team visibility, and tie every feature or service you present directly back to their business outcomes.

To execute the Align stage successfully and ensure you are building a compelling business case for expansion, modern CS teams rely on three interconnected motions: Goal Facilitation, Sharing Insights, and the Alignment Meeting.

Goal Facilitation: Navigating the "Messy Middle" Once a customer achieves their First Value, they enter what is known as the "messy middle". This is the critical time period between their first use of your product and their first renewal, where true relationships are built and ultimate outcomes are decided. If you are missing from the messy middle, your customers can easily hit a wall, give up, and ultimately churn.

To prevent this, you must practice Goal Facilitation. Goal Facilitation ensures that every recommendation or action you take aligns directly with the customer’s long-term vision. By staying actively involved, monitoring progress, and identifying gaps, you help customers overcome obstacles and accelerate their path to success.

A highly effective way to facilitate goals is to break massive objectives down into smaller, bite-sized "Value Blocks," much like building a Lego set. Each Goal Block acts like a single Lego packet with clear, distinct steps and a simple path to success. By completing one small Goal Block at a time, the customer maintains a cadence of progress and never feels overwhelmed.

Sharing Insights: The Rudder on the Ship As customers work through their goals, you must ensure their progress is recognized. Sharing Insights is a simple yet powerful strategy that ensures your recommendations, successes, and lessons learned are actively communicated within the customer’s organization,.

Think of Sharing Insights as the rudder on a ship—you use it to steer your customer toward the value they desire to achieve. By sharing insights proactively, you build organizational awareness, establish proof points for future advocacy, and create internal momentum for future expansions.

To ensure your insights do not land flat, modern CS teams use a highly digestible, five-step formula:

  • Write a one-liner: Convey the gist of the insight to grab the reader's attention,.
  • Speak to the background: Bring them into the fold so the insight makes sense to the uninitiated,.
  • Speak to the results: Clearly illustrate the outcomes (good or bad) and show what is possible,.
  • Clarify the action: Translate the results into something actionable so the reader knows exactly what to do next,.
  • Reinforce the insight: Tell them what you told them by bringing it back to the start,.

The Alignment Meeting: Killing the Passive QBR The final component of the Align stage is formalizing your partnership. The days of vague, passive, checkbox-style Quarterly Business Reviews (QBRs) are over. Often, companies go into traditional QBRs and spend the entire time talking about themselves—their new features, their roadmap, and their company.

To scale growth, these must be replaced with active Alignment Meetings. Alignment Meetings shift the focus from static, company-centric reviews to dynamic, customer-centric discussions that address real-time challenges and strategic goals.

During an Alignment Meeting, your objective is to:

  • Review past activity and wins: Contextualize their accomplishments and celebrate the Value Blocks they have completed.
  • Reaffirm the Five Questions: Go back through the exact questions asked during the Kickoff to find out if their answers are still the same, or if their business dynamics have changed.
  • Drive Expansion Conversations: Look beyond the immediate renewal to identify new opportunities, exploring additional use cases or adjacent teams that could benefit from your solution.

When you proactively facilitate their goals, share insights that prove your value, and hold structured Alignment Meetings, you cement your role as a strategic partner. This continuous alignment makes the decision to renew or expand a logical, frictionless next step.
