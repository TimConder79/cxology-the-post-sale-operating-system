Stage 3 — Advocate

You cannot close a deal alone. You need an internal champion who can navigate the organization for you.

Even with the most compelling business case and a track record of perfect alignment, a Customer Success Manager cannot force an expansion or renewal across the finish line alone. The third stage of the post-sales pipeline is Advocate. To drive internal support, secure consensus, and build momentum for growth, you must intentionally map, engage, and empower internal champions.

Advocacy is built on trust and alignment, transforming your primary point of contact from a passive user of your product into a true champion for your solution. Without strong advocates, internal roadblocks can delay—or completely stall—an otherwise great expansion opportunity.

Formal Stakeholder Mapping To build true Client Intimacy, you cannot rely on an informal approach to managing your customer's organizational chart. You must implement formal Stakeholder Mapping to identify key players, understand their level of influence in decision-making, and formulate a targeted growth plan.

Begin by categorizing individual client stakeholders into specific roles, such as Steering Committee Member, Assists with Issues, Influencer, or Approves Renewal.

Next, you must define your relationship position by assessing their attitude toward your company, assigning a strict communication objective to each:

  • Sponsor: A stakeholder who fully advocates for our program.
  • Update: A supporter who can provide us with valuable information.
  • Educate: A stakeholder who lacks understanding and cannot yet be a supporter.
  • Neutralize: A detractor who works against the program.

As you map these stakeholders, take the time to analyze their personal styles and motivations—such as their need to excel, take action, analyze data, or drive consensus—so you can adjust your messaging and communication details accordingly.

Scripting the Communication Event Plan Once your stakeholders are mapped, you must define the details of your upcoming communication events. Think of this like an offensive coordinator in American football. In football, the first 15 to 20 plays are "scripted" to create rhythm within the offense, identify how the defense will react to different formations, and fine-tune the game plan.

Likewise, CSMs must deliberately script a Communication Event Plan for each key stakeholder over a multi-week timeline. By deliberately planning out your communication cadence, you ensure that each meeting with key stakeholders adds value and advances the relationship. You must define the specific goal of each event, the content needed to achieve that outcome, and who will deliver the story in a way that creates a desire for the stakeholder to take action.

Arming the Advocate: Proof Points and Insights You cannot send your advocate into an internal budget meeting empty-handed. To help them influence decision-makers and secure buy-in, you must arm them with undeniable proof of value.

This is where the strategy of Sharing Insights becomes your most powerful pipeline tool. You must actively equip advocates with tailored data, success stories, and measurable outcomes to demonstrate the ongoing value of your solution. When your advocate forwards your impact report to their CFO with a note saying, "Look at the ROI we achieved this quarter," you have effectively outsourced your selling motion to the most persuasive person in the room.

Leveraging the Alignment Meeting Your structured Alignment Meetings are the ideal venue to put your scripted communication plan into action and put your advocate center stage. Use these sessions to validate the business case, address any lingering objections, and secure broader internal buy-in.

These meetings provide an opportunity to engage advocates directly in front of their executive team, using their insights and influence to further align your solutions with the company's broader business goals. By reinforcing their role during these high-stakes touchpoints, you turn them into active participants in driving the success of the partnership.
