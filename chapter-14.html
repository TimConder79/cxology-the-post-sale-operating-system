Play 6 — The Sharing Insights Play

Sharing Insights is the rudder on the ship. It steers your customer toward the value they desire to achieve.

As your customer works through their Value Blocks, you must actively steer them toward success, ensure they don't veer off course, and continuously prove the value of your partnership. This is accomplished through the Sharing Insights Play.

If Value Blocks represent the engine driving your customer forward, Sharing Insights is the rudder on the ship. This play is how you communicate additional value, celebrate wins, correct missteps, and expand your reach within the customer’s organization.

1. The Why: The Rudder on the Ship

In the "messy middle" of the customer journey, Customer Success Managers often experience a frustrating phenomenon: you provide key advice, but it feels like you are talking into a microphone that is turned off. Your customers might be doing the required actions, but they aren't optimizing for the outcome, or worse, they are resisting the change required to fully adopt your solution.

Sharing Insights is your mechanism to break through the noise. It operates as a critical change management tool, helping guide users through the natural human resistance to new technology (often mapped to the Kubler-Ross change curve) and accelerating them toward commitment.

When executed correctly, a quality insight does four things:

  • Expands your reach: It gives your primary contact a tangible artifact to forward to their executive team, increasing your visibility.
  • Cements shared achievements: It formally recognizes and documents the value achieved during a Value Block.
  • Informs and teaches better practices: It corrects bad habits before they become ingrained.
  • Sets the stage for growth: It naturally positions upsells or partner offerings by highlighting gaps that need filling.

Without a structured play, insights often land flat. This usually happens because the CS team sends a visual that only makes sense to an expert, fails to meet the customer where they are, or delivers the insight too late to be actionable.

2. Design & Build: The 5-Step Formula

To ensure your insights are not ignored, you must build this play around a simple, repeatable formula. Whether you are sharing an insight about feature utilization, a better practice, or a massive win, your team must use this highly digestible, five-step structure:

  • Step 1: Write a one-liner. Convey the gist of the insight immediately to grab the reader's attention so they continue reading.
  • Step 2: Speak to the background. Bring the reader into the fold. Provide the necessary context so the insight makes perfect sense, even to an uninitiated executive.
  • Step 3: Speak to the results. Clearly illustrate the outcomes (good or bad) and show what is possible. If you are sharing a chart or graph, this is where it goes.
  • Step 4: Clarify the action. Translate the results into something actionable. The reader must know exactly what you want them to do next.
  • Step 5: Reinforce the insight. Tell them what you told them by bringing it back to the start, reinforcing the core message.

Mapping the Triggers When building this play in your CS automation tool or CRM, you must define the triggers for when these insights are sent. Insights should be deployed:

  • After a Value Block completion: To send a "Win Report" celebrating the milestone.
  • During stalled usage: To point out a better practice when a customer hits a roadblock or usage drops.
  • On an automated cadence: Sending automated performance or usage insights every two to four weeks to keep the customer engaged.

3. Best Practices: Digestibility and Internal Sharing

To master the Sharing Insights Play, implement these critical best practices:

  • Keep it Fun-Sized: Use consistent formatting across all your insight types and ensure they are "bite-sized nuggets". You must minimize the work required for the customer to understand the insight and, more importantly, act on it.
  • Label Graphics for the Uninitiated: When sharing a chart, a graph, or a screen capture to illustrate your results, always highlight exactly where you want the reader to look. Make sure the graphics are labeled clearly so that an executive who has never logged into your platform can immediately understand the value.
  • Keep an Internal Learning Library: Do not let great insights disappear into a single email thread. Keep a library of well-written examples. This helps new team members see how it is done and provides inspiration on days when a CSM is struggling to write the perfect insight.
  • Share Wins Internally: Routinely recognize great insights in your internal team meetings. Share customer "Win Reports" with the entire company. There are team members in your company (like engineering or product) who never work directly with a customer and benefit immensely from seeing the positive impact of their work. Furthermore, Sales and Marketing love these stories, quickly turning them into hot case studies and reference materials.

By consistently executing the Sharing Insights play, you steer the customer effortlessly through the messy middle, ensuring they are perfectly primed for your next major inflection point.
