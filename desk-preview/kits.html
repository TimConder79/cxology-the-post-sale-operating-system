We're a mid-market SaaS company. One of our larger customers is at $180K ARR, 14 months in, and renewal is in 5 months. They bought 4 modules but only adopted 2, and usage of the main one is down about 30% since the summer. Our champion, their Director of Ops, got promoted and handed us to a newer manager without much pull. The VP who signed hasn't met with us since kickoff. They've cancelled their last two QBRs, had layoffs, and their CFO is reviewing every contract over $100K. Sales wants to pitch our new analytics add-on. What should we do?

What's really going on

This renewal is at the Renewal and Growth Decision Inflection Point, and the case was never built. Your champion moved on, and the replacement is a friendly contact rather than an advocate. A friendly contact is not the same as an advocate.1 With cancelled QBRs and a CFO reviewing contracts, a champion can believe in the product and still lose the internal business case if usage is unclear or outcomes are vague.2

The play to run

Key Contact Change comes first: if you do not proactively define the narrative, the new contact may view your software as an unnecessary expense.3 Then Renew & Grow: work backward from how the customer buys, not forward from the contract date.4 Hold the analytics pitch. This customer needs to stabilize before it is ready to grow.4

Your next 3 moves

1. Run the "You - We - Me" meeting with the new manager. Start entirely focused on them: their new role, successes, challenges, and milestones.3 Ask the promoted Director to help get the VP back in the room.Applied to your situation
2. Build your renewal readiness pack. A Renewal Readiness Brief, a Usage & License Review, and a Stakeholder & Approval Map that includes the CFO.4 Two unused modules can be a clean chance to right-size and rebuild trust.4
3. Align with Sales before anyone contacts the customer. The customer should experience one team.4 Log the reporting tickets as a signal for later, not a pitch.Applied to your situation

How you'll know it's working

You return to Advocate when your primary contact can explain the business case to their leadership without you present.5 You reach Intent when the budget owner, approval sequence, procurement steps, and target date are known.5
